## 1. SnSe thermoelectric record (recommended first backup)

**Paste into Cassation:**
> Is the record thermoelectric figure of merit ZT = 2.6 at 923 K reported for SnSe single crystals an intrinsic property of the material, or an artifact of low-density samples?

**Why genuinely contested — the two sides, in Nature itself:**
- *Claim:* Zhao et al., "Ultralow thermal conductivity and high thermoelectric
  figure of merit in SnSe crystals", Nature 508, 373 (2014) — ZT = 2.6 ± 0.3 at
  923 K along the b axis, then a world record, hinging on ultralow lattice
  thermal conductivity. [doi:10.1038/nature13184](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13184)
- *Counter:* Wei et al., "The intrinsic thermal conductivity of SnSe", Nature 539,
  E1 (2016) — the Zhao samples were only ~88–90 % of theoretical density; fully
  dense SnSe crystals show markedly higher thermal conductivity, so the reported
  κ (and hence ZT) is not intrinsic.
  [doi:10.1038/nature19832](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature19832) ·
  [PDF](https://www.phys.sinica.edu.tw/~lowtemp/publication/2016%20nature19832.pdf)
- *Rebuttal:* Zhao et al. reply, Nature 539, E2 (2016) — lattice κ is ultralow
  regardless of whether density is 6.0 or 5.5 g/cm³.
  [doi:10.1038/nature19833](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature19833)
- *Aftermath:* independent "fully dense single-crystalline SnSe" measurements
  report higher κ / lower ZT ([OSTI 1506789](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/1506789)),
  while the same group later claimed ZT ≈ 3.1 in polycrystals (2021) — the fight
  never fully settled.

**Why stage-legible:** "a material that turns waste heat into electricity at
record efficiency" needs zero background; the dispute is one headline number
(2.6) versus one physical, explainable artifact (a 10 %-porous sample), and the
takeover quote pair is a published Nature comment-and-reply — primary sources
don't get cleaner.

**Expected dispute for the Skeptic:** Advocate finds ZT 2.6/Nature 2014 and its
thousands of citations; Skeptic finds the Nature 2016 Brief Communications
Arising on sample density and the fully-dense remeasurements. Clerk routes
"intrinsic vs artifact" to CONTESTED; jury rules between the comment and the
reply. Deterministic in rehearsal — this controversy is in review articles, so
grounded search reliably surfaces both sides.

**NEO tie:** thermoelectrics are exactly the class of energy-materials numbers a
screening pipeline would gate spend on.

## 2. The A-Lab's "41 new materials" (most on-story; one staging caveat)

**Paste into Cassation:**
> Did Berkeley's autonomous A-Lab discover and synthesize 41 previously unknown inorganic materials in 17 days, as reported in Nature in 2023?

**Why genuinely contested:**
- *Claim:* Szymanski et al., "An autonomous laboratory for the accelerated
  synthesis of inorganic materials", Nature 624, 86 (2023) — robotic lab,
  ML-predicted targets, "41 novel compounds" headline.
  [doi:10.1038/s41586-023-06734-w](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06734-w)
- *Counter:* Leeman, Liu, Stiles, Lee, Bhatt, Schoop & Palgrave, "Challenges in
  High-Throughput Inorganic Materials Prediction and Autonomous Synthesis",
  PRX Energy 3, 011002 (2024) — most "novel" products are misidentified known
  phases or compositionally disordered variants already in the ICSD; XRD
  refinements don't support the novelty claims.
  [UCL open-access PDF](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10188733/2/Palgrave_Challenges%20in%20High-Throughput%20Inorganic%20Materials%20Prediction%20and%20Autonomous%20Synthesis_VoR.pdf) ·
  [NSF PAR record](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10532478)
- *Resolution-in-progress:* Nature issued an author correction (Jan 2026): title
  changed from "novel materials" to "inorganic materials", headline count reframed
  to 36 compounds from 57 targets — and reporting says questions remain.
  [C&EN, Jan 2026](https://cen.acs.org/research-integrity/Nature-robot-chemist-paper-corrected/104/web/2026/01) ·
  [Chemistry World](https://www.chemistryworld.com/news/new-analysis-raises-doubts-over-autonomous-labs-materials-discoveries/4018791.article) ·
  [correction, PMC12872444](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12872444/)

**Why stage-legible:** it's the most famous autonomous-science claim of the
decade, the dispute fits one sentence ("new to science, or already in the
database?"), and it even has a third act — a Nature correction nine months old —
so the verdict brief gets to show SUPPORTED, REFUTED *and* HUMAN_ADJUDICATED
chips on one board.

**Expected dispute for the Skeptic:** novelty of specific compounds vs ICSD
matches and disorder misassignment; the January 2026 correction as the
tie-breaker evidence the re-planned agent should find.

**Staging caveat (decide before offering it):** the A-Lab's predicted targets
came from the GNoME lineage, and the same critique ecosystem (Leeman/Palgrave;
Cheetham & Seshadri in Chem. Mater.) also targets DeepMind's GNoME claims.
Cross-examining it in front of DeepMind judges is high-risk/high-reward: framed
as "Cassation surfaces the sourced disagreement — including about our own field's
headlines", it's the gutsiest possible proof of neutrality; framed carelessly it
reads as a jab. Keep the question worded about the *A-Lab synthesis* claims
(Berkeley), never about GNoME. Best held for the judge-question lane in Q&A
rather than the main demo.

**NEO tie:** this IS the NEO story — trusting an AI materials-discovery pipeline's
claimed discoveries before spending real synthesis/compute budget.

## 3. Graphene thermal conductivity (same-domain spare)

**Paste into Cassation:**
> Does suspended single-layer graphene really have a room-temperature thermal conductivity of ~5300 W/mK, above diamond, as originally reported?

**Why genuinely contested:**
- *Claim:* Balandin et al., "Superior Thermal Conductivity of Single-Layer
  Graphene", Nano Lett. 8, 902 (2008) — 5300 ± 480 W/mK by Raman optothermal
  measurement; one of the most-cited numbers in nanoscience.
  [paper record](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a68d501c2c1b292e558a7c80d34906b400f0c799)
- *Counter:* later suspended-graphene measurements land far lower — ~1800 W/mK
  near 325 K (Lee et al., [arXiv:1103.3337](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.3337));
  reexamination of the basal-plane value
  ([OSTI 1185992](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/1185992)); the gap
  traces to the assumed optical absorption and neglect of non-equilibrium
  phonons in the original Raman method.
- *State of play:* "Is Thermal Conductivity of Graphene Divergent and Higher Than
  Diamond?" ([arXiv:2302.12216](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12216)) — the field
  still argues about both the value and whether κ even converges with sample
  length ([length-dependence, arXiv:1404.5379](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.5379)).

**Why stage-legible:** "the most famous number in nanotech is probably 2–3×
too high" is a one-line hook; everyone on a mixed panel has heard graphene
superlatives.

**Expected dispute for the Skeptic:** 5300 (2008) vs ~1800–2700 (2010s
remeasurements), plus the methodological kill-shot (absorption assumption).
Caveat: it is the same observable as the main BAs question — good news for
calibration transfer (the tuned Skeptic prompt behaves identically), weak news
for demonstrating breadth. Hence: spare, not primary backup.
